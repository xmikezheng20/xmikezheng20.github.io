I am a PhD student in the lab of [Dr. Arkarup Banerjee](https://www.cshl.edu/research/faculty-staff/arkarup-banerjee/) at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory. I am broadly interested in the neural basis of animal behavior, particularly how neural circuits evolve to generate new behaviors. In the Banerjee lab, my research focuses on vocal communication in [Alston's singing mice (*S. teguina*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alston%27s_brown_mouse). Singing mice produce both the ultrasonic vocalizations (USVs) typical of rodents and the loud, multi-second songs unique to their lineage. Using a combination of behavioral quantification, viral tools, optogenetics, and in vivo electrophysiology, my work characterized the singing mouse vocal repertoire and investigated the role of the midbrain periaqueductal gray (PAG) in controlling both ancestral and novel vocalizations.

Before graduate school, I completed a B.A. in neuroscience and computer science at Colby College.
